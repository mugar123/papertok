Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026 (revision 13).

PaperTok is an application for discovering and reading scientific research. This page describes what data is collected, why, where it is kept and who it is shared with. It is written from what the code actually does, not from a template.

In short

  • You can use PaperTok without an account. The feed, search and the public pages work signed out. Usage analytics uses a persistent browser identifier unless you turn it off.
  • Usage analytics is on by default. It uses cookies and local storage to connect visits across days and links product activity to your account when you sign in. You can turn it off in Settings or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the screen. Sessions are not recorded.
  • Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers that make the app work handle your data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from Settings → Account → Delete account. The feed order the server keeps for you is not deleted with it; it expires on its own, at most 2 hours after your last page.
  • For anything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

Who is responsible

PaperTok is a personal project, not a company. The data controller is its author, and the contact for anything to do with this data is nicomg60@gmail.com.

You can use PaperTok without an account

The paper feed, search and the public pages work without signing in. Without an account no account profile is created. Usage analytics can still connect visits from the same browser, as described under Analytics.

What is collected if you create an account

Sign-in is with Google or GitHub. PaperTok never sees or stores your password: Firebase Authentication handles the sign-in and only the result arrives here.

This data is stored in Google Firestore. The legal basis is performing the service you ask for when you create the account: without it there is no library and no personalised feed.

An AI summary is saved only once it is finished, one per paper and reading level. Only your account can read your summaries. Write it again replaces the saved summary when the new one finishes. Summaries are kept until you delete your account.

A paper counts as viewed when its card stays on your screen for at least one second or when you skip it. Besides your interactions with each paper, your account keeps a list of the last 500 papers you viewed, so that the feed on the other devices where you sign in does not show them again. Once the list is full, each new view pushes the oldest one off the list. A second list keeps the last 1,200 highly cited papers the feed offered you that you viewed, so that it does not offer them again. Only your account can read these lists.

Your account also counts, for the highly cited papers and for the recent papers in your feed, how many you were shown and how many likes, saves and PDF opens they got. A PDF open counts only when the paper opens in PaperTok's PDF reader, not when it opens on another site, such as the publisher's or your library's. Skipping a paper or marking it Not interested adds no like, save or open. Once 2,000 papers shown are counted, every count is halved, so your recent use weighs as much as all the use before it. Your feed keeps between one place in fifteen and one in three for a highly cited paper you have not seen. Each page picks its share at random, weighted by those counts; when the feed has no such paper to put in a place, the next paper takes it. The feed receives at most five highly cited papers at a time, so a full page holds at most about one in five on the first page and one in seven on later pages. Your account also keeps one number between −1 and 1 that likes, saves and long reads of very recent or older papers move, and that orders the feed toward recent or older papers. A long read of such a paper moves it every time, even when you read that paper again. Your account keeps the last 200 papers whose like, save or PDF open moved the counts or that number, so that a paper's like, save or PDF open counts once, and an unlike or unsave takes back what the like or save added. Only your account can read them. None of this is stored for a visitor without an account. A tab or app on any device still running an older version of PaperTok does not keep the counts, the number or the 200 papers. Using it saves your interactions without them, so they can start again from zero. Reloading or closing it ends this. Otherwise it looks for the new version every few minutes while on screen, and switches when you next open another page.

If you link a university, Firebase emails a verification link to your university address. This browser temporarily stores that address and the pending request, scoped to your PaperTok account. The request expires after one hour and is cleared on its next use. Cancellation, account changes and completion also clear it. An address given on the welcome, before you have an account, waits in this browser with your other welcome answers. It is sent once your account is created and cleared then, or when you log out first. Your profile stores the verified institution, not the secondary address. A temporary Firebase proof account is deleted after verification where possible; an existing account is preserved.

With a verified email, your saves count by default. PaperTok links your account to each paper you save once per UTC week. Turn this off in Settings → Privacy to exclude future saves. Deleting your account removes your contributions for weeks that have yet to close.

Collection begins on the first Monday after launch and covers weeks from Monday to Sunday in UTC. It counts signed-in accounts with a verified email when PaperTok recognizes the paper's identifier. To limit abuse, the server briefly keeps a request count under a hashed account identifier that changes each minute. PaperTok keeps contributions in Firestore. A temporary receipt records the paper and server time after a save succeeds. PaperTok removes the receipt after sending it for counting and schedules automatic deletion if the browser closes first. PaperTok may look up the public paper identifier in OpenAlex. PostHog receives no contributions or counters for author statistics. PaperTok is collecting contributions before making any counts available to authors. When weekly totals become available, closing a week will leave only the total per paper, without account links.

What is public and what is not

By default your activity is private. The only thing you can make public now is a list, by publishing it. A published list can be reached by anyone with the link, search engines included. You can stop sharing it at any time from My lists; from then on it is no longer served, although a search engine may take a while to update its copy.

Comments and public profiles from earlier versions of PaperTok are no longer shown to anyone, although a search engine may take a while to drop a copy it made earlier.

University email verification

When you request a university link, PaperTok sends that email address to Firebase Authentication to generate a one-time verification link and to Resend to deliver it. The pending request stays in your browser for up to one hour. Cloudflare stores hashed account and recipient identifiers for request limits and a delivery status; it does not store the verification link or message body. Your linked institution records the university and email domain, not the full address. Requesting verification does not subscribe you to email updates.

Zotero

Connecting Zotero takes you to zotero.org, where you allow PaperTok to read and write your personal Zotero library, including its notes. Group libraries are not included. Zotero then gives PaperTok an access key, which is stored encrypted and is only used when you press Send to Zotero while reading a paper. Each send gives Zotero the paper's citation (title, authors, date, identifiers, abstract and link) and the highlights and notes you made on that paper, as one note. Highlights made by the AI are not sent. The note labels each quote as coming from the paper's original text or from a specific reading level of PaperTok's AI summary. If the source is unknown, the note says so. Disconnecting in Settings → Profile deletes the key, and you can also revoke it at zotero.org. What you already sent stays in your Zotero library.

ORCID

Connecting ORCID from My profile takes you to orcid.org, where you sign in and allow PaperTok to read your ORCID iD. ORCID then gives PaperTok your iD and the name on your ORCID record, and PaperTok keeps the two with your account; only your account can read them. ORCID also gives an access token, which PaperTok does not store. While you are on orcid.org, PaperTok keeps a hash of a one-time value that works for 15 minutes; connecting, disconnecting or deleting your account deletes it. My profile lists the public works of your ORCID record and is shown only to you. Your browser loads those works from ORCID's public API directly, so ORCID receives your IP address and your iD. The details of each work with a DOI come from OpenAlex through PaperTok's server, like other lookups. Under them, My profile suggests works that OpenAlex links to your iD and that your ORCID record does not list, also asked of OpenAlex through PaperTok's server with your iD. OpenAlex can link a work to the wrong person. When you mark a suggested work as yours, it joins your publications on My profile; when you mark it as not yours, it stops being suggested. PaperTok keeps the work's identifier (its DOI, or its OpenAlex id when it has none) in one of those two lists with your account; only your account can read them, and your ORCID record does not change. My profile also lists papers from the last 12 months that cite your publications there: it asks OpenAlex through PaperTok's server, with the OpenAlex ids of up to 50 of them, for the newest 20 such papers, and leaves out those OpenAlex lists with your iD among their authors and your publications listed there. It also lists the coauthors you share two or more of those works with, counted from the works it has already loaded. PaperTok keeps neither list; following a coauthor there adds them to your follows, as following any author does. My profile suggests feed interests from the topics of your loaded publications. You can review them in the interests editor. Only saving that selection updates your feed interests. Disconnecting in My profile deletes your iD, your name and those two lists from your account. PaperTok stays among the trusted organizations in your ORCID account settings until you remove it there.

Email updates

If you turn them on, PaperTok emails you what is new from the authors, topics, institutions and projects you follow, or, while you follow none of them, the notable new papers in the subjects you chose. If you connect your ORCID iD, the weekly email can also list new papers that cite your work, even if you follow nothing. PaperTok sends your iD and the OpenAlex work identifiers it checks to OpenAlex. It uses the works OpenAlex links to your iD and your current “This is mine” and “Not mine” decisions. PaperTok stores your email address, your sending preferences, what you follow, the identifiers of the subjects you chose and an unsubscribe identifier in Cloudflare Workers KV or, as it moves, in a Cloudflare Durable Object of your account, and your account identifier and that unsubscribe identifier in Cloudflare Workers KV. If a sending limit delays an edition, PaperTok also stores its selected papers, abstracts and reasons they matched your follows or subjects, along with any selected citing papers and the time you connected ORCID. That selection is removed after delivery, a change to your follows or a later edition that is processed; otherwise its delivery record expires after 120 days. That record also keeps the last publication date a citation lookup covered and the week of the latest lookup, so a late email or a retry does not skip papers. Resend sends the emails. To limit test emails, Cloudflare counts them under a hashed account identifier that it keeps for up to three days. To limit citation lookups, Cloudflare counts one lookup per week under a hashed account identifier that expires at the end of that UTC week.

The legal basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it whenever you like: every email carries a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can also turn updates off in Settings. Unsubscribing deletes the subscription.

Analytics

Usage analytics is enabled by default. PaperTok uses PostHog, hosted in the European Union (Frankfurt), to understand repeat visits and product use. PostHog keeps a random identifier in a first-party cookie and local storage on papertok.app. This identifier connects activity from the same browser across days. When you sign in, PaperTok links that activity to your account identifier. Signing out or switching accounts starts a new browser identity.

Product events record the papers you view, open, like, save, highlight or annotate by their identifier, how long cards and the reader stay on screen, the authors, topics or institutions you follow by their identifier, search types and result counts, and the outcome of sign-in and AI explanation requests. When you open a paper in the app's viewer, they record whether it showed the paper's text (and from which archive), its PDF, a link or nothing, why a PDF could not be shown, and how long the viewer stays on screen. Starting to read a paper aloud records only that it happened, not which paper. Product events also record when you open a paper's connections or its similar papers, and what you do in the connections map, such as switching to the list or centring the graph on another paper. Creating or opening one of your feeds records the order it uses; creating one also records how many areas or topics, authors and keywords it holds. Deleting one records only that it happened. No event records a feed's name or what is in it. Sending a paper to Zotero records only whether it was sent, not which paper. With usage analytics on, PaperTok records once per session if you like, save or read a paper while signed in before your recommendation profile loads. This measurement records only that it happened. Your browser keeps the last session sampled so it can skip repeat actions. With usage analytics on, PaperTok links survey events to your account. When the survey opens and when you submit or dismiss it, those events include your reading level and broad areas of interest, such as Physics, when available. They omit the narrower topics you picked. The events also record the app version and roughly how long ago this browser's first real use was, in days or weeks. Submitting the survey records your answers, including your role if you chose one. Dismissing it records only that you closed it alongside the event details above. The survey has no free text. Feed diagnostics record load starts, duration, result, initial loads versus refreshes or additional pages, automatic and manual retries, and the web and server versions. They contain no paper identifiers or interests. Events include the browser and its version, the operating system and its version, the device type, and session and tab identifiers. They also carry a short browser label built only from the browser, its major version and the operating system (for example "Chrome/130 (Mac OS X)"), so PostHog can tell people from automated traffic; this label never contains the full user agent your browser sends.

PaperTok does not send your name, email address, search text, notes, paper titles or full page addresses to product analytics. Page addresses have query strings, fragments and individual paper, list, feed and profile identifiers removed. PaperTok does not record sessions or automatically capture clicks or heatmaps.

Vercel Web Analytics separately counts page views and countries. It uses no cookies and its visitor identifier expires each day. Page addresses sent to Vercel are stripped of identifiers and query strings too.

You can turn off both services in Settings → Privacy or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the welcome screens and of the public pages a shared link opens: a paper, a list, an author, institution, journal, topic or project page, and the page for a link that no longer works. The choice is kept in this browser. Turning analytics off stops new browser events and removes PostHog's stored browser identity; previously queued events may still be delivered. On subsequent visits neither analytics library loads. A previous rejection of detailed tracking remains disabled until you turn analytics on. To request deletion of previously collected data, write to the contact address below.

Error reports from the browser and backend also go to PostHog. Reports contain a standard error type, code locations, software version and fixed labels. Browser reports use the same identity and stripped page addresses as product events. When the feed fails to load, its error event carries the failure reason, the HTTP status and the error code. Error messages, paper content, notes and account profile details are removed. Turning analytics off stops new browser error reports. Backend reports contain no reader identity and continue independently of this choice.

PostHog's requests go to papertok.app itself, and Vercel relays them to PostHog. PostHog keeps events for one year. Your browser sends its IP address to connect to these services; removing it from an event payload does not hide the connection's IP address from the receiving service.

Scientific sources and AI features

Searches and papers come from public repositories: OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar, CORE, OSTI, NASA ADS, INSPIRE, OpenReview, Hugging Face, Unpaywall and OpenCitations, among others.

Those queries do not leave from your browser: they go through an intermediate server on Cloudflare, so those repositories never receive your IP address from a search or lookup (figures and PDFs are the exception, described below). That server counts requests to respect each source's limits. For a signed-in reader it also counts them per account, under a hashed account identifier it keeps for up to three days.

When the in-app reader requests a paper's text or its PDF, an AI assistant sends a request to PaperTok's MCP server, or a public request looks up papers, paper figures, open-access links or a shared page, that server uses the IP address supplied by Cloudflare to limit requests and keep one visitor from exhausting the shared download allowance. It stores a protected identifier derived from the address and the current minute using a secret key, together with request counts. For an IPv6 address it uses the network prefix (the first 64 bits) instead of the full address, and an IPv4 address written in IPv6 form counts as that IPv4 address. Lookups that go through OpenAlex also keep a second identifier, derived the same way from the address and the current day, so that one address cannot use up the day's shared OpenAlex allowance: each address has 1,000 billed units and 3,000 lookups by identifier per UTC day. Only requests the server cannot answer from a copy it already holds count against these allowances. It does not store the original IP address in these counters or attach your account or the paper you requested. The per-minute identifier changes each minute and the daily one each day; neither is sent to analytics. People sharing a public IP, such as a campus network, share each route’s allowance. The PDF reader allows 30 requests per UTC minute, and the MCP server 30 tool calls.

The text of a paper comes through that server too, but its figures do not: your browser loads each figure, and a PDF shown in the reader on a computer, directly from the repository that hosts it (arXiv, PubMed Central or Europe PMC), which receives your IP address as with any image on the web.

Pages for an author, an institution, a journal or a topic, and an author's preview in the reader, ask Wikidata, Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons from your browser for a short summary and a picture of what they show, and the picture loads from Wikimedia's servers. Wikimedia receives your IP address and the name or identifier looked up, as with any visit to Wikipedia; PaperTok sends nothing about your account.

These PDF counters are stored in Cloudflare Durable Objects. Their automatic deletion is scheduled for one minute after the counting window ends, at most two minutes after a request. They can remain longer if Cloudflare delays or fails to run that cleanup, but expired counters do not affect later windows. The arXiv, paper-figure and open-access lookup counters use the same cleanup schedule, and so do the MCP server's counters. OpenAlex counters, both the per-minute and the daily identifiers, and shared-page lookup counters are stored with the day's allowance, except the counters for lookups by identifier: those sit in 16 separate stores a day that hold only the per-address counts. Each store's deletion is scheduled for at most three days after the first request it receives, and Cloudflare can run that deletion late.

Your feed comes from a shared catalogue that server keeps: once a day it collects new papers from those repositories, the same for every reader. Each request for more papers sends it your interests in groups, each with a weight and the subjects whose likes may rank it. The weights reflect your likes, saves and long reads and the new subjects you added. The groups leave out subjects you removed. The request also sends what you follow, the identifiers of up to 24 papers you liked, saved or read at length, and a compact filter of the papers you viewed or were already shown, and of the papers it offered that you had already liked, saved, read or dismissed, which the server checks papers against to offer only papers you have not seen; liking, saving or opening a paper also asks it for papers related to that one, by its identifier. They are used to choose and rank that page. So the feed can go on from where you stopped, the server keeps the order it ranked for you, and how far down it you got, for up to 2 hours after your last page. It is kept in the cache of the Cloudflare data center where that server runs (placed near Frankfurt; Cloudflare may move it to a nearby data center), under a key derived from your account, and is not copied elsewhere. It holds the papers of that order, not the list of papers you liked, saved or read at length. Deleting your account does not remove it sooner; it expires on its own. Apart from that order, which papers you liked, saved, opened or viewed is not stored there, and neither is the filter. A request for a page of one of your own feeds sends instead that feed's areas, categories, research topics, authors, journals and keywords, the identifiers of up to 24 of the papers you most recently liked, saved or read at length, and the identifiers of the papers you removed from that feed, so the page leaves them out. Until you refresh the feed, it sends no filter of the papers you viewed. Once you refresh it, its pages also send a compact filter of the papers you viewed and of the papers that feed had already shown you, so the feed starts with the papers you have not seen. Papers you marked Not interested are left out by your browser. A paper you liked, saved or opened that the catalogue does not have yet is added to it without any link to you. An author, institution, topic, journal, search or project you follow or put in one of your feeds is added to the list of things the catalogue collects papers for, also without any link to you, and leaves that list within 16 days of the last time anybody asks for it, later only if the daily clean-up fails. Cloudflare Workers AI turns the titles and abstracts of the catalogue's papers into numeric vectors. When papers you liked, saved or opened have no vector yet, their titles and abstracts are sent to it together, without your identity.

Paper explanations and rewrites are generated by a Claude language model, run by Anthropic. Anthropic may process the request in any region it serves, including outside the European Union. That model receives the paper's text and the language and level you choose, never your identity or your email.

When you listen to a paper in the reader, the text of each paragraph is sent to Cartesia, in the United States, which turns it into speech. When Cartesia cannot answer, the text goes instead to ElevenLabs, also in the United States, which keeps the text in its request history. Both receive the text, never your identity or your email. PaperTok's server stores the audio, with the time each word is spoken when ElevenLabs read it, and plays it to anyone who listens to the same paragraph. When neither can answer, your browser's own voice reads the paragraph instead. Your browser also keeps a copy of the paragraphs you have listened to, for up to 30 days and at most 800 paragraphs, so a paper opens again at once. The copy belongs to the account that listened: another account signed in on the same browser does not use it, and it is deleted once that account listens to something.

Each account may have two new papers a day read in this voice. To count them, the server keeps, per UTC day, how many new papers and characters of new audio your account used, and for each paper that needed new audio a counter named by a hash of the day, your account and the paper. These counters are kept for three days and are not sent to analytics. When every paragraph of a paper has stored audio, the server also marks that paper, without your account, so the feed can show its audio is ready.

Who it is shared with

Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers needed for the application to work are involved:

  • Google (Firebase Authentication, Firestore) — accounts and app data.
  • Vercel — hosting, page-view analytics, and relaying analytics requests to PostHog.
  • PostHog — product-use analytics and error reports, on servers in the European Union.
  • Cloudflare (Workers, KV, Durable Objects, R2) — the intermediate server, email-update storage and read-aloud audio.
  • Cartesia and ElevenLabs — the paragraph text they turn into speech when you listen to a paper in the reader.
  • Resend — sending the emails.
  • Anthropic — writing the AI summaries and passage explanations, from the paper's text.
  • Zotero — only if you connect it: the papers and highlights you send to your library.
  • ORCID — only if you connect it: signing in there to confirm your iD, and the public works of your ORCID record, which your browser loads from ORCID directly.
  • arXiv, PubMed Central, Europe PMC and Wikimedia — figures, PDFs, and Wikipedia summaries and pictures that your browser loads from them directly.

How long it is kept

Your account data is kept for as long as the account exists. You can delete it from Settings → Account → Delete account. That deletes your lists, your highlights, your AI summaries, what you follow, your interactions with papers and the lists of papers you viewed, your preferences, the record of the latest policy announcement you were shown, the email subscription, your Zotero connection, your ORCID connection with the works you marked on My profile, and the sign-in account, along with what earlier versions of PaperTok kept for you: your public profile, the photo you uploaded, the people you followed, your comments, the content reports you filed and those filed about your comments. The feed order the server keeps for you (see Scientific sources and AI features) is not deleted with the account: that cache cannot be searched by account, so the order expires on its own, at most 2 hours after your last page.

The request counters this policy describes, whether kept under a hashed account identifier, a protected identifier derived from an IP address or a hashed recipient identifier, are stored in Cloudflare Durable Objects. The times given for them are when PaperTok deletes each counter from that storage. Cloudflare keeps a recovery history of that storage that reaches back 30 days, and its documentation does not say that deleting a counter removes it from that history, so a deleted counter may remain there for up to 30 days more. PaperTok has no feature that reads that history; it exists to restore the storage after a failure.

Your rights

You can access, correct and delete your data, object to its processing, ask for it to be restricted and request a copy in a portable format. Much of this you can do from within the app, in Settings. For everything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

If you believe the processing is not correct, you can lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, Spain's data protection authority.

Storage in your browser

PaperTok keeps a few things in your own browser, and no server reads them: the light or dark theme, the language, your choice about analytics and how many times it has changed, a session cache so the application does not reload from scratch when you come back, the last 500 papers your account viewed in this browser, or highly cited papers the feed passed over because you had already liked, saved, read or dismissed them, so a reload skips them, and the metadata for papers in your lists and favorites so their categories can appear without waiting for the network after a reload, and the marks analytics uses to count a first visit per session and a first real use. Only your browser keeps the mark for a return 7 to 13 days after your first real use. PaperTok measures these returns using the first-use and visit events already sent to PostHog. Clearing site data removes these browser records. Signing out also clears the viewed papers and list metadata. Turning analytics off clears the analytics marks. This browser also keeps, for each of your own feeds, the latest 200 papers you removed from it, under your account identifier. Unlike the things above, these are sent to the server with each request for that feed, so the feed leaves them out. They are cleared when you delete that feed in this browser, when you sign out and when you clear the site's data. For the short survey, this browser keeps under your account identifier the last three days you opened PaperTok, with the app version of each day, and, until the survey is first shown, up to twenty papers you kept on screen for at least a second, so the survey waits until you have used the app. They stay in this browser, and they are cleared when you sign out and when you clear the site's data. It also keeps the app version you were last shown the survey for, so this browser shows it once per version; that one stays in this browser too, also after you sign out, and is removed when you clear the site's data or delete the account from Settings in this browser. While usage analytics is enabled, PostHog also keeps an identifier and your choice in a cookie and in local storage, as described under Analytics. When the browser blocks the Google or GitHub sign-in window, the tab goes to the provider and comes back; during that trip this tab's session storage briefly holds the provider, the part of the app that asked and the page to return to, and they are removed when the page comes back or when the browser restores it after Back. This browser also keeps, under your account identifier, a copy of the latest policy version whose announcement your account was shown, so it does not ask your account again, also after you sign out and in again. Clearing the site's data removes the copy, and so does deleting the account from Settings in this browser.

This browser saves your welcome interests until they are saved to a new account, an account with existing interests loads, or you sign out or switch accounts. It also keeps a copy of your signed-in interest preferences under your account identifier so they are ready when you return. Signing out clears that copy.

Changes

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, the date at the top will be updated. Until PaperTok 0.4, you will see the current announcement in the app if you sign in to an account created before the change took effect and have no record of seeing an announcement from 7 November 2026 or later.
