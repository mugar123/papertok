Privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026.

PaperTok is an application for discovering and reading scientific research. This page describes what data is collected, why, where it is kept and who it is shared with. It is written from what the code actually does, not from a template.

In short

  • You can use PaperTok without an account. The feed, search and the public pages work signed out. Usage analytics uses a persistent browser identifier unless you turn it off.
  • Usage analytics is on by default. It uses cookies and local storage to connect visits across days and links product activity to your account when you sign in. You can turn it off in Settings or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the screen. Sessions are not recorded.
  • Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers that make the app work handle your data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from Settings → Account → Delete account. The feed order the server keeps for you is not deleted with it; it expires on its own, at most 2 hours after your last page.
  • For anything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

Who is responsible

PaperTok is a personal project, not a company. The data controller is its author, and the contact for anything to do with this data is nicomg60@gmail.com.

You can use PaperTok without an account

The paper feed, search and the public pages work without signing in. Without an account no account profile is created. Usage analytics can still connect visits from the same browser, as described under Analytics.

What is collected if you create an account

Sign-in is with Google or GitHub. PaperTok never sees or stores your password: Firebase Authentication handles the sign-in and only the result arrives here.

This data is stored in Google Firestore. The legal basis is performing the service you ask for when you create the account: without it there is no library and no personalised feed.

A plain-words summary is saved only once it is finished, one per paper and reading level. Only your account can read your summaries. Write it again replaces the saved summary when the new one finishes. Summaries are kept until you delete your account.

A paper counts as viewed when its card stays on your screen for at least one second or when you skip it. Besides your interactions with each paper, your account keeps a list of the last 500 papers you viewed, so that the feed on the other devices where you sign in does not show them again. Once the list is full, each new view pushes the oldest one off the list. A second list keeps the last 1,200 highly cited papers the feed offered you that you viewed, so that it does not offer them again. Only your account can read these lists.

If you link a university, Firebase emails a verification link to your university address. This browser temporarily stores that address and the pending request, scoped to your PaperTok account. The request expires after one hour and is cleared on its next use. Cancellation, account changes and completion also clear it. An address given on the welcome, before you have an account, waits in this browser with your other welcome answers. It is sent once your account is created and cleared then, or when you log out first. Your profile stores the verified institution, not the secondary address. A temporary Firebase proof account is deleted after verification where possible; an existing account is preserved.

What is public and what is not

By default your activity is private. The only thing you can make public now is a list, by publishing it. A published list can be reached by anyone with the link, search engines included. You can stop sharing it at any time from My lists; from then on it is no longer served, although a search engine may take a while to update its copy.

Comments and public profiles from earlier versions of PaperTok are no longer shown to anyone, although a search engine may take a while to drop a copy it made earlier.

University email verification

When you request a university link, PaperTok sends that email address to Firebase Authentication to generate a one-time verification link and to Resend to deliver it. The pending request stays in your browser for up to one hour. Cloudflare stores hashed account and recipient identifiers for request limits and a delivery status; it does not store the verification link or message body. Your linked institution records the university and email domain, not the full address. Requesting verification does not subscribe you to email updates.

Email updates

If you turn them on, PaperTok emails you what is new from what you follow. For that it stores your email address, your sending preferences and an unsubscribe identifier in Cloudflare Workers KV. If a sending limit delays an edition, PaperTok also stores its selected papers, abstracts and reasons they matched your follows. That selection is removed after delivery, a change to your follows or a later edition that is processed; otherwise its delivery record expires after 120 days. Resend sends the emails. To limit test emails, Cloudflare counts them under a hashed account identifier that it keeps for up to three days.

The legal basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it whenever you like: every email carries a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can also turn updates off in Settings. Unsubscribing deletes the subscription.

Analytics

Usage analytics is enabled by default. PaperTok uses PostHog, hosted in the European Union (Frankfurt), to understand repeat visits and product use. PostHog keeps a random identifier in a first-party cookie and local storage on papertok.app. This identifier connects activity from the same browser across days. When you sign in, PaperTok links that activity to your account identifier. Signing out or switching accounts starts a new browser identity.

Product events record the papers you view, open, like, save, highlight or annotate by their identifier, how long cards and the reader stay on screen, the authors, topics or institutions you follow by their identifier, that you followed a journal but not which one, search types and result counts, and the outcome of sign-in and AI explanation requests. They also record when you open a paper's connections or its similar papers, and what you do in the connections map, such as switching to the list or centring the graph on another paper. Events include browser, operating system, device type and session identifiers.

PaperTok does not send your name, email address, search text, notes, paper titles or full page addresses to product analytics. Page addresses have query strings, fragments and individual paper, list and profile identifiers removed. PaperTok does not record sessions or automatically capture clicks or heatmaps.

Vercel Web Analytics separately counts page views and countries. It uses no cookies and its visitor identifier expires each day. Page addresses sent to Vercel are stripped of identifiers and query strings too.

You can turn off both services in Settings → Privacy or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the welcome screens and of the public pages a shared link opens: a paper, a list, an author, institution, journal, topic or project page, and the page for a link that no longer works. The choice is kept in this browser. Turning analytics off stops new browser events and removes PostHog's stored browser identity; previously queued events may still be delivered. On subsequent visits neither analytics library loads. A previous rejection of detailed tracking remains disabled until you turn analytics on. To request deletion of previously collected data, write to the contact address below.

Error reports from the browser and backend also go to PostHog. Reports contain a standard error type, code locations, software version and fixed labels. Browser reports use the same identity and stripped page addresses as product events. When the feed fails to load, its error event carries the failure reason, the HTTP status and the error code. Error messages, paper content, notes and account profile details are removed. Turning analytics off stops new browser error reports. Backend reports contain no reader identity and continue independently of this choice.

PostHog's requests go to papertok.app itself, and Vercel relays them to PostHog. PostHog keeps events for one year. Your browser sends its IP address to connect to these services; removing it from an event payload does not hide the connection's IP address from the receiving service.

Scientific sources and AI features

Searches and papers come from public repositories: OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar, CORE, OSTI, NASA ADS, INSPIRE, OpenReview, Hugging Face, Unpaywall and OpenCitations, among others.

Those queries do not leave from your browser: they go through an intermediate server on Cloudflare, so those repositories never receive your IP address. That server counts requests to respect each source's limits. For a signed-in reader it also counts them per account, under a hashed account identifier it keeps for up to three days.

When the in-app PDF reader requests a document, or a public request looks up papers, paper figures, open-access links or a shared page, that server uses the IP address supplied by Cloudflare to limit requests and keep one visitor from exhausting the shared download allowance. It stores a protected identifier derived from the address and the current minute using a secret key, together with request counts. It does not store the original IP address in these counters or attach your account or the paper you requested. The identifier changes each minute and is not sent to analytics. People sharing a public IP, such as a campus network, share each route’s allowance. The PDF reader allows 30 requests per UTC minute.

These PDF counters are stored in Cloudflare Durable Objects. Their automatic deletion is scheduled for one minute after the counting window ends, at most two minutes after a request. They can remain longer if Cloudflare delays or fails to run that cleanup, but expired counters do not affect later windows. The arXiv, paper-figure and open-access lookup counters use the same cleanup schedule. OpenAlex and shared-page lookup counters are stored with their daily allowance and are scheduled for deletion after three days.

Your feed comes from a shared catalogue that server keeps: once a day it collects new papers from those repositories, the same for every reader. Each request for more papers sends it your interests in groups, each with a weight that gives a subject you added recently more of the page and the subjects whose likes may rank it, which leave out the subjects you removed, what you follow, the identifiers of up to 24 papers you liked, saved or read at length, and a compact filter of the papers you viewed or were already shown, and of the papers it offered that you had already liked, saved, read or dismissed, which the server checks papers against to offer only papers you have not seen; liking, saving or opening a paper also asks it for papers related to that one, by its identifier. They are used to choose and rank that page. So the feed can go on from where you stopped, the server keeps the order it ranked for you, and how far down it you got, for up to 2 hours after your last page. It is kept in the cache of the Cloudflare data center where that server runs (placed near Frankfurt; Cloudflare may move it to a nearby data center), under a key derived from your account, and is not copied elsewhere. It holds the papers of that order, not the list of papers you liked, saved or read at length. Deleting your account does not remove it sooner; it expires on its own. Apart from that order, which papers you liked, saved, opened or viewed is not stored there, and neither is the filter. A paper you liked, saved or opened that the catalogue does not have yet is added to it without any link to you. An author, institution, topic, journal, search or project you follow is added to the list of things the catalogue collects papers for, also without any link to you, and leaves that list within 16 days of the last time anybody asks for it, later only if the daily clean-up fails. Cloudflare Workers AI turns the titles and abstracts of the catalogue's papers into numeric vectors. When papers you liked, saved or opened have no vector yet, their titles and abstracts are sent to it together, without your identity.

Paper explanations and rewrites are generated by an open language model, DeepSeek V4 Flash, run by NaN Builders on servers in the European Union. That model receives the paper's text and the language and level you choose, never your identity or your email.

Who it is shared with

Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers needed for the application to work are involved:

  • Google (Firebase Authentication, Firestore) — accounts and app data.
  • Vercel — hosting, page-view analytics, and relaying analytics requests to PostHog.
  • PostHog — product-use analytics and error reports, on servers in the European Union.
  • Cloudflare (Workers, KV) — the intermediate server and email-update storage.
  • Resend — sending the emails.

How long it is kept

Your account data is kept for as long as the account exists. You can delete it from Settings → Account → Delete account. That deletes your lists, your highlights, your plain-words summaries, what you follow, your interactions with papers and the lists of papers you viewed, your preferences, the email subscription and the sign-in account, along with what earlier versions of PaperTok kept for you: your public profile, the photo you uploaded, the people you followed, your comments, the content reports you filed and those filed about your comments. The feed order the server keeps for you (see Scientific sources and AI features) is not deleted with the account: that cache cannot be searched by account, so the order expires on its own, at most 2 hours after your last page.

Your rights

You can access, correct and delete your data, object to its processing, ask for it to be restricted and request a copy in a portable format. Much of this you can do from within the app, in Settings. For everything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

If you believe the processing is not correct, you can lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, Spain's data protection authority.

Storage in your browser

PaperTok keeps a few things in your own browser, and no server reads them: the light or dark theme, the language, your choice about analytics, a session cache so the application does not reload from scratch when you come back, the last 500 papers your account viewed in this browser, or highly cited papers the feed passed over because you had already liked, saved, read or dismissed them, so a reload skips them, and the marks analytics uses to count a first visit per session, a first real use and a return a week later once each. They are cleared when you clear the site's data, the viewed papers also when you sign out, and the analytics marks also when you turn analytics off. While usage analytics is enabled, PostHog also keeps an identifier and your choice in a cookie and in local storage, as described under Analytics. When the browser blocks the Google or GitHub sign-in window, the tab goes to the provider and comes back; during that trip this tab's session storage briefly holds the provider, the part of the app that asked and the page to return to, and they are removed when the page comes back or when the browser restores it after Back.
