Privacy policy

Last updated 17 October 2026.

PaperTok is an application for discovering and reading scientific research. This page describes what data is collected, why, where it is kept and who it is shared with. It is written from what the code actually does, not from a template.

In short

  • You can use PaperTok without an account. The feed, search and the public pages work signed out, and nothing that identifies you is stored.
  • Analytics uses no cookies and never records which paper you read or what you searched for. You can turn it off in Settings or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the screen.
  • Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers that make the app work handle your data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from Settings → Account → Delete account.
  • For anything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

Who is responsible

PaperTok is a personal project, not a company. The data controller is its author, and the contact for anything to do with this data is nicomg60@gmail.com.

You can use PaperTok without an account

The paper feed, search and the public pages work without signing in. Without an account no profile is created, and the only record of your visit is the anonymous measurement described under Analytics.

What is collected if you create an account

Sign-in is with Google or GitHub. PaperTok never sees or stores your password: Firebase Authentication handles the sign-in and only the result arrives here.

This data is stored in Google Firestore. The legal basis is performing the service you ask for when you create the account: without it there is no library and no personalised feed.

What is public and what is not

By default your activity is private. The only thing you can make public now is a list, by publishing it. A published list can be reached by anyone with the link, search engines included. You can stop sharing it at any time from My lists; from then on it is no longer served, although a search engine may take a while to update its copy.

Comments and public profiles from earlier versions of PaperTok are no longer shown to anyone, although a search engine may take a while to drop a copy it made earlier.

Email updates

If you turn them on, PaperTok emails you what is new from what you follow. For that it stores your email address, your sending preferences and an unsubscribe identifier, in Cloudflare Workers KV. Brevo sends the emails.

The legal basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it whenever you like: every email carries a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can also turn updates off in Settings. Unsubscribing deletes the subscription.

Analytics

PaperTok measures how the application is used with two services, and neither uses cookies. Vercel Web Analytics counts page views and the country a visit comes from: to tell visits apart, Vercel derives an identifier from the browser itself that expires every day and cannot follow you from one day to the next or across sites. PostHog, on its servers in the European Union (Frankfurt), counts what is done in the application. It keeps nothing about you in your browser: to tell visits apart, it combines your IP address and your browser's user agent with a random value that changes every day, keeps only the result and discards the IP address and the user agent, and it derives no location from them. Neither identifier is linked to your account.

The legal basis is PaperTok's legitimate interest in knowing which parts of the application are used, so they can be improved. That is why you are not asked for permission before measuring. You can object whenever you like by turning it off in Settings → Privacy or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the welcome screens and the feed. The choice is kept in this browser and turns off both services: from then on nothing new is recorded from it, and from your next visit neither library is even loaded.

What is sent is the page you are on, with the path stripped of identifiers, to both services, and to PostHog a fixed list of actions, each with a few fixed values. Reading a specific paper is recorded as /public/paper/:id, and visiting an author's profile as /explorer/author/:id: which one never travels. A search is recorded as a search, with its type and how many results it returned, never with what you searched for. When the application stops with an error or the feed cannot load, PostHog receives only which kind of failure happened, never the error itself. Each event also carries the type of device, the browser and the operating system. Nothing from your account is sent.

PostHog's requests go to papertok.app itself, and Vercel relays them to PostHog. PostHog keeps the events for one year.

Scientific sources and AI features

Searches and papers come from public repositories: OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar, CORE, OSTI, NASA ADS, INSPIRE, OpenReview, Hugging Face, Unpaywall and OpenCitations, among others.

Those queries do not leave from your browser: they go through an intermediate server on Cloudflare, so those repositories never receive your IP address. That server logs the volume of requests, to respect each source's limits, not who makes them.

Paper explanations and rewrites are generated by an open language model, DeepSeek V4 Flash, run by NaN Builders on servers in the European Union. That model receives the paper's text and the language and level you choose, never your identity or your email.

Who it is shared with

Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers needed for the application to work are involved:

  • Google (Firebase Authentication, Firestore) — accounts and app data.
  • Vercel — hosting, page-view analytics, and relaying analytics requests to PostHog.
  • PostHog — analytics of what is done in the application, on servers in the European Union.
  • Cloudflare (Workers, KV) — the intermediate server and email-update storage.
  • Brevo — sending the emails.

How long it is kept

Your account data is kept for as long as the account exists. You can delete it from Settings → Account → Delete account. That deletes your lists, your highlights, what you follow, your preferences, the email subscription and the sign-in account, along with what earlier versions of PaperTok kept for you: your public profile, the photo you uploaded, the people you followed, your comments, the content reports you filed and those filed about your comments.

Your rights

You can access, correct and delete your data, object to its processing, ask for it to be restricted and request a copy in a portable format. Much of this you can do from within the app, in Settings. For everything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

If you believe the processing is not correct, you can lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, Spain's data protection authority.

Storage in your browser

PaperTok keeps a few things in your own browser, and no server reads them: the light or dark theme, the language, your choice about analytics, a session cache so the application does not reload from scratch when you come back, and the marks analytics uses to count a first visit per session, a first real use and a return a week later once each, without knowing who you are. They are cleared when you clear the site's data, and the analytics marks also when you turn analytics off.

Changes

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, the date at the top will be updated and the change will be announced in the application itself.
