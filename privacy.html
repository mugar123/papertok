Privacy policy

Last updated 17 October 2026.

PaperTok is an application for discovering and reading scientific research. It is not meant for people under 16. This page describes what data is collected, why, where it is kept and who it is shared with. It is written from what the code actually does, not from a template.

In short

  • You can use PaperTok without an account. The feed, search and the public pages work signed out, and nothing that identifies you is stored.
  • Analytics sets no tracking cookies and never records which paper you read or what you searched for. You can turn it off in Settings or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the screen.
  • Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers that make the app work handle your data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from Settings → Account → Delete account.
  • For anything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

Who is responsible

PaperTok is a personal project, not a company. The data controller is its author, and the contact for anything to do with this data is nicomg60@gmail.com.

You can use PaperTok without an account

The paper feed, search and the public pages work without signing in. Without an account no profile is created, and the only record of your visit is the anonymous measurement described under Analytics.

What is collected if you create an account

Sign-in is with Google or GitHub. PaperTok never sees or stores your password: Firebase Authentication handles the sign-in and only the result arrives here.

This data is stored in Google Firestore. The legal basis is performing the service you ask for when you create the account: without it there is no library and no personalised feed.

What is public and what is not

By default your activity is private. The only thing you can make public now is a list, by publishing it. A published list can be reached by anyone with the link, search engines included. You can stop sharing it at any time from My lists; from then on it is no longer served, although a search engine may take a while to update its copy.

Comments and public profiles from earlier versions of PaperTok are no longer shown to anyone, although a search engine may take a while to drop a copy it made earlier.

Email updates

If you turn them on, PaperTok emails you what is new from what you follow. For that it stores, in Cloudflare Workers KV, your email address and name, a copy of what you follow with a few recent papers from it, your sending preferences, an unsubscribe identifier, the papers already sent to you (so none is sent twice) and whether the last email went out. Brevo sends the emails and receives your address and name to do so. If you ask for them on the welcome screens, your account keeps that answer until you follow something, and then the emails start.

The legal basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it whenever you like: every email carries a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can also turn updates off in Settings. Unsubscribing deletes all of it.

Analytics

PaperTok measures how the application is used with two services, and neither sets cookies. Vercel Web Analytics counts page views and the country a visit comes from: to tell visits apart, Vercel derives an identifier from the browser itself that expires every day and cannot follow you from one day to the next or across sites. PostHog, on its servers in the European Union (Frankfurt), counts what is done in the application. It keeps nothing about you in your browser: to tell visits apart, it combines your IP address and your browser's user agent with a random value that changes every day, keeps only the result and discards the IP address and the user agent, and it derives no location from them. Neither identifier is linked to your account.

The legal basis is PaperTok's legitimate interest in knowing which parts of the application are used, so they can be improved. That is why you are not asked for permission before measuring. You can object whenever you like by turning it off in Settings → Privacy or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the welcome screens and the feed. The choice is kept in this browser, in its storage and in a preference cookie, papertok_analytics_decision, which holds only that choice and lasts a year. Turning analytics off stops both services: from then on nothing new is recorded from this browser, and from your next visit neither library is even loaded.

What is sent is the page you are on, with the path stripped of identifiers, to both services, and to PostHog a fixed list of actions, each with a few fixed values. Reading a specific paper is recorded as /public/paper/:id, and visiting an author's profile as /explorer/author/:id: which one never travels. A search is recorded as a search, with its type and how many results it returned, never with what you searched for. Each event also carries the type of device, the browser and the operating system. Nothing from your account is sent.

PostHog's requests go to papertok.app itself, and Vercel relays them to PostHog. PostHog keeps the events for one year.

Scientific sources and AI features

Searches and papers come from public repositories: OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar, CORE, OSTI, NASA ADS, INSPIRE, OpenReview, Hugging Face, Unpaywall and OpenCitations, among others.

Most requests go through an intermediate server on Cloudflare, so the repositories behind them do not receive your IP address. That includes searches for papers, authors and topics, and the papers in your feed. Some lookups go straight from your browser to the service that answers them, and that service receives your IP address and what was asked, as any website you visit does:

  • figure images of arXiv papers, from arXiv;
  • datasets and software linked to a paper, from DataCite;
  • details of PubMed papers, from Europe PMC;
  • research projects, from OpenAIRE: the project behind a paper, project pages and their papers, and project searches, which send what you typed;
  • institutions and authors, from ROR, ORCID, Wikidata, Wikipedia and Crossref, including institution searches, which send what you typed;
  • a PDF you open on a computer, from the site that hosts it.

The Cloudflare server counts requests to stay within each source's limits. Some features need an account, such as AI rewrites, publishing a list, related papers and a few sources; for those it counts requests per account and deletes the counts within three days.

Plain-language rewrites of a paper, and explanations of a passage you select in a rewrite, are generated by an open language model, DeepSeek V4 Flash, run by NaN Builders on servers in the European Union. For a rewrite, Cloudflare Workers AI first turns the paper's PDF into text; the model receives that text (or the PDF itself, when no text can be extracted), the paper's title, year and DOI, and the level you choose. For a passage, it receives the passage, its paragraph, the paper's title and the level. It never receives your identity, your email or your IP address.

Who it is shared with

Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers needed for the application to work are involved:

  • Google (Firebase Authentication, Firestore) — accounts and app data.
  • Vercel — hosting, page-view analytics, and relaying analytics requests to PostHog.
  • PostHog — analytics of what is done in the application, on servers in the European Union.
  • Cloudflare (Workers, KV, Workers AI) — the intermediate server, email-update storage, and turning a paper's PDF into text for a rewrite.
  • NaN Builders — generating rewrites and passage explanations, on servers in the European Union. It receives paper text and the passages you select, never who asked.
  • Brevo — sending the emails.

How long it is kept

Your account data is kept for as long as the account exists. You can delete it from Settings → Account → Delete account. That deletes your lists and the public copies of any you published, your saved papers, your highlights, your likes, skips and reading history, what you follow, your preferences, your verified university, the email subscription and the sign-in account, along with what earlier versions of PaperTok kept for you: your public profile, the photo you uploaded, the people you followed, your comments, the content reports you filed and those filed about your comments.

Your rights

You can access, correct and delete your data, object to its processing, ask for it to be restricted and request a copy in a portable format. Much of this you can do from within the app, in Settings. For everything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

If you believe the processing is not correct, you can lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, Spain's data protection authority.

Storage in your browser

PaperTok keeps a few things in your own browser, and nothing on the server uses them: the light or dark theme, your answers to the welcome screens, your choice about analytics (also kept in the preference cookie described under Analytics), a session cache so the application does not reload from scratch when you come back, and the marks analytics uses to count a first visit per session, a first real use and a return a week later once each, without knowing who you are. They are cleared when you clear the site's data, and the analytics marks also when you turn analytics off.

Changes

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, the date at the top will be updated and the change will be announced in the application itself.
