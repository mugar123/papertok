Privacy policy

Last updated 24 October 2026.

PaperTok is an application for discovering and reading scientific research. This page describes what data is collected, why, where it is kept and who it is shared with. It is written from what the code actually does, not from a template.

In short

  • You can use PaperTok without an account. The feed, search and the public pages work signed out, and nothing that identifies you is stored.
  • By default, analytics uses no cookies and never records which paper you read or what you searched for. If you have an account and allow detailed analytics, what you do in PaperTok is linked to your account and your sessions are recorded. You can change either in Settings or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the screen.
  • Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers that make the app work handle your data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from Settings → Account → Delete account.
  • For anything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

Who is responsible

PaperTok is a personal project, not a company. The data controller is its author, and the contact for anything to do with this data is nicomg60@gmail.com.

You can use PaperTok without an account

The paper feed, search and the public pages work without signing in. Without an account no profile is created, and the only record of your visit is the anonymous measurement described under Analytics.

What is collected if you create an account

Sign-in is with Google or GitHub. PaperTok never sees or stores your password: Firebase Authentication handles the sign-in and only the result arrives here.

This data is stored in Google Firestore. The legal basis is performing the service you ask for when you create the account: without it there is no library and no personalised feed.

A plain-words summary is saved only once it is finished, one per paper and reading level. Only your account can read your summaries. Write it again replaces the saved summary when the new one finishes. Summaries are kept until you delete your account.

A paper counts as viewed when its card stays on your screen for at least one second or when you skip it. Besides your interactions with each paper, your account keeps a list of the last 500 papers you viewed, so that the feed on the other devices where you sign in does not show them again. Once the list is full, each new view pushes the oldest one off the list. A second list keeps the last 1,200 papers you viewed on a card marked as highly cited, so that the feed does not offer them again. Only your account can read these lists.

If you link a university, Firebase emails a verification link to your university address. This browser temporarily stores that address and the pending request, scoped to your PaperTok account. The request expires after one hour and is cleared on its next use. Cancellation, account changes and completion also clear it. An address given on the welcome, before you have an account, waits in this browser with your other welcome answers. It is sent once your account is created and cleared then, or when you log out first. Your profile stores the verified institution, not the secondary address. A temporary Firebase proof account is deleted after verification where possible; an existing account is preserved.

What is public and what is not

By default your activity is private. The only thing you can make public now is a list, by publishing it. A published list can be reached by anyone with the link, search engines included. You can stop sharing it at any time from My lists; from then on it is no longer served, although a search engine may take a while to update its copy.

Comments and public profiles from earlier versions of PaperTok are no longer shown to anyone, although a search engine may take a while to drop a copy it made earlier.

University email verification

When you request a university link, PaperTok sends that email address to Firebase Authentication to generate a one-time verification link and to Resend to deliver it. The pending request stays in your browser for up to one hour. Cloudflare stores hashed account and recipient identifiers for request limits and a delivery status; it does not store the verification link or message body. Your linked institution records the university and email domain, not the full address. Requesting verification does not subscribe you to email updates.

Email updates

If you turn them on, PaperTok emails you what is new from what you follow. For that it stores your email address, your sending preferences and an unsubscribe identifier in Cloudflare Workers KV. If a sending limit delays an edition, PaperTok also stores its selected papers, abstracts and reasons they matched your follows. That selection is removed after delivery, a change to your follows or a later edition that is processed; otherwise its delivery record expires after 120 days. Resend sends the emails.

The legal basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it whenever you like: every email carries a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can also turn updates off in Settings. Unsubscribing deletes the subscription.

Analytics

PaperTok measures how the application is used with two services: Vercel Web Analytics and PostHog, on PostHog's servers in the European Union (Frankfurt). There are two levels. Anonymous analytics is on for everybody unless they turn it off. Detailed analytics is off until you allow it.

Anonymous analytics

Neither service uses cookies at this level. Vercel Web Analytics counts page views and the country a visit comes from: to tell visits apart, Vercel derives an identifier from the browser itself that expires every day and cannot follow you from one day to the next or across sites. PostHog counts what is done in the application. It keeps nothing about you in your browser: to tell visits apart, it combines your IP address and your browser's user agent with a random value that changes every day, keeps only the result and discards the IP address and the user agent, and it derives no location from them. Neither identifier is linked to your account.

What is sent is the page you are on, with the path stripped of identifiers, to both services, and to PostHog a fixed list of actions, each with a few fixed values. Reading a specific paper is recorded as /public/paper/:id, and visiting an author's profile as /explorer/author/:id: which one never travels. A search is recorded as a search, with its type and how many results it returned, never with what you searched for. Each event also carries the type of device, the browser and the operating system. Nothing from your account is sent.

The legal basis is PaperTok's legitimate interest in knowing which parts of the application are used, so they can be improved. That is why you are not asked for permission before measuring. You can object whenever you like by turning it off in Settings → Privacy or, without an account, with the Privacy button at the top of the welcome screens and the feed. The choice is kept in this browser and turns off both services: from then on nothing new is recorded from it, and from your next visit neither library is even loaded.

Detailed analytics

When you are signed in, PaperTok asks whether you allow detailed analytics. If you choose Accept, or turn on Detailed analytics in Settings → Privacy, PostHog links what you do in the application to your account, so that PaperTok can see how each person uses it over time. It then records:

  • your account identifier, your email address, your name and how you sign in;
  • the full address of each page, including which paper or profile it shows and what you typed into search;
  • the papers you see, open, like, save, highlight or annotate, with their identifier and title, and what you search for;
  • how long each paper stays on screen and how long you spend in the reader;
  • the buttons and links you click, with their text, and where you click and scroll on a page;
  • recordings of your sessions: what the page shows, and your mouse movements, clicks and scrolling. Text typed into fields is hidden in the recordings;
  • errors the application runs into and how fast pages load;
  • an approximate location, the country and city, which PostHog derives from the IP address of each request.

At this level PostHog keeps an identifier in a first-party cookie and in your browser's local storage on papertok.app, so it can recognise the same browser from one visit to the next. PostHog may also show you a short survey inside the application.

The legal basis is your consent. You can withdraw it whenever you like in Settings → Privacy, by turning detailed analytics off: from then on PostHog goes back to anonymous analytics and nothing new is linked to your account. Signing out also ends the link for that browser. To have what was already recorded deleted, write to the address below.

We also record whether the reader opens, whether a rewrite completes, its duration and whether it came from a saved version, and whether a sign-in attempt succeeds, fails or is cancelled.

Error reports from the browser and from our backend also go to PostHog. With anonymous analytics, a browser report contains a standard error type, the places in PaperTok's code where the error happened, the software version and a few fixed labels, with the stripped page address and device details described above. Error messages, paper content, notes, search text and account details are removed before sending. With detailed analytics, reports also include the error message and the full page address. Turning analytics off stops new browser error reports. Backend reports contain only the error type, code locations, software version and fixed labels, carry nothing about the reader and continue whatever the analytics choice, to help us operate and repair the service.

PostHog's requests go to papertok.app itself, and Vercel relays them to PostHog. PostHog keeps events for one year, and session recordings for at most as long.

Scientific sources and AI features

Searches and papers come from public repositories: OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar, CORE, OSTI, NASA ADS, INSPIRE, OpenReview, Hugging Face, Unpaywall and OpenCitations, among others.

Those queries do not leave from your browser: they go through an intermediate server on Cloudflare, so those repositories never receive your IP address. That server counts requests to respect each source's limits. For a signed-in reader it also counts them per account, under a hashed account identifier it keeps for up to three days.

When the in-app PDF reader requests a document, that server uses the IP address supplied by Cloudflare to limit requests and keep one visitor from exhausting the shared download allowance. It stores a protected identifier derived from the address and the current minute using a secret key, together with request counts. It does not store the original IP address in these counters or attach your account or the paper you requested. The identifier changes each minute and is not sent to analytics. People sharing a public IP, such as a campus network, share the limit of 30 requests per UTC minute.

These PDF counters are stored in Cloudflare Durable Objects. Their automatic deletion is scheduled for one minute after the counting window ends, at most two minutes after a request. They can remain longer if Cloudflare delays or fails to run that cleanup, but expired counters do not affect later windows.

Your feed comes from a shared catalogue that server keeps: once a day it collects new papers from those repositories, the same for every reader. Each request for more papers sends it your interests, what you follow, the identifiers of up to 24 papers you liked or saved, and a compact filter of the papers you viewed or were already shown, and of the papers it offered that you had already liked, saved, read or dismissed, which the server checks papers against to offer only papers you have not seen; liking, saving or opening a paper also asks it for papers related to that one, by its identifier. They are used to choose and rank that page; which papers you liked, saved, opened or viewed is not stored there, and neither is the filter. A paper you liked, saved or opened that the catalogue does not have yet is added to it without any link to you. An author, institution, topic, search or project you follow is added to the list of things the catalogue collects papers for, also without any link to you, and leaves that list within 16 days of the last time anybody asks for it, later only if the daily clean-up fails. Cloudflare Workers AI turns the titles and abstracts of the catalogue's papers into numeric vectors. When papers you liked, saved or opened have no vector yet, their titles and abstracts are sent to it together, without your identity.

Paper explanations and rewrites are generated by an open language model, DeepSeek V4 Flash, run by NaN Builders on servers in the European Union. That model receives the paper's text and the language and level you choose, never your identity or your email.

Who it is shared with

Nothing is sold and nothing is shared for advertising. Only the providers needed for the application to work are involved:

  • Google (Firebase Authentication, Firestore) — accounts and app data.
  • Vercel — hosting, page-view analytics, and relaying analytics requests to PostHog.
  • PostHog — analytics of what is done in the application, error reports and, if you allow detailed analytics, session recordings, on servers in the European Union.
  • Cloudflare (Workers, KV) — the intermediate server and email-update storage.
  • Resend — sending the emails.

How long it is kept

Your account data is kept for as long as the account exists. You can delete it from Settings → Account → Delete account. That deletes your lists, your highlights, your plain-words summaries, what you follow, your interactions with papers and the lists of papers you viewed, your preferences, the email subscription and the sign-in account, along with what earlier versions of PaperTok kept for you: your public profile, the photo you uploaded, the people you followed, your comments, the content reports you filed and those filed about your comments.

Your rights

You can access, correct and delete your data, object to its processing, ask for it to be restricted and request a copy in a portable format. Much of this you can do from within the app, in Settings. For everything else, write to nicomg60@gmail.com.

If you believe the processing is not correct, you can lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, Spain's data protection authority.

Storage in your browser

PaperTok keeps a few things in your own browser, and no server reads them: the light or dark theme, the language, your choice about analytics, a session cache so the application does not reload from scratch when you come back, the last 500 papers your account viewed in this browser, or highly cited papers the feed passed over because you had already liked, saved, read or dismissed them, so a reload skips them, and the marks analytics uses to count a first visit per session, a first real use and a return a week later once each, without knowing who you are. They are cleared when you clear the site's data, the viewed papers also when you sign out, and the analytics marks also when you turn analytics off. If you allow detailed analytics, PostHog also keeps an identifier and your choice in a cookie and in local storage, as described under Analytics.
